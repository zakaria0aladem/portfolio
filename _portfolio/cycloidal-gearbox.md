# High-Precision Cycloidal Gearbox Design and Kinematic Analysis using SolidWorks<br>
<br>

<br>
## Overview<br>
Designed a fully functional cycloidal drive from scratch, a specialized speed reducer known for high reduction ratios, compact form factor, and low backlash. The mechanism is engineered for high-accuracy applications such as robotic actuators and precision machine tools.<br>
<br>
## Mechanism & Working Principle<br>
The design operates on a kinematic principle where a high-speed input shaft drives an eccentrically mounted bearing. This eccentric motion forces the cycloidal disc to roll along the internal circumference of stationary ring gear rollers.<br>
<br>
Due to the geometry where the disc has one fewer lobe than the number of ring gear rollers, a full rotation of the input shaft causes the disc to advance by a single lobe distance in the reverse direction, producing a high reduction ratio.<br>
<br>
The reduction behavior follows the relationship where n represents the number of lobes on the disc and N represents the number of rollers on the ring gear.<br>
<br>
## Engineering Challenges & Solutions<br>
<br>
### Parametric Profile Generation<br>
The core of the design relies on the precise geometry of the cycloidal disc. The tooth profile is based on an epitrochoid curve, traced by a point at a defined distance from the center of a circle rolling around another circle.<br>
<br>
Parametric equations were implemented in SolidWorks to generate the cycloidal profile, ensuring smooth contact mechanics and accurate force transmission between the disc lobes and ring gear rollers.<br>
<br>
### Vibration Dampening (Double Disc Architecture)<br>
Single-disc cycloidal drives suffer from vibration due to eccentric mass rotation at high speeds. To address this, a double cycloidal drive configuration was implemented.<br>
<br>
- Two identical cycloidal discs stacked and offset by 180 degrees out of phase.<br>
- Internal force balancing achieved through phase offset, significantly reducing vibration.<br>
- Improved smoothness and stability at high rotational speeds.<br>
